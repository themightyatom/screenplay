# LANGÅ — CHARACTER LIST (v1)

Working list of everyone the source material names or implies. Two lines each. Ages are as of November 1943.
★ = survived the war. † = died as a direct result of the action.
Sources: research notes, Anders Straarup's article (RAHS 2020), Knud Birk's memoir pages, the Randers Kommune info board.

---

## A. THE SIX ON THE BRIDGE — night of 17 November 1943

**KAI HANNIBAL HOFF, 30–31 — "Carlo"** †
Adjunct teacher at Randers Statsskole, Winter War veteran from the Finnish army, runs the Terrain Sports Club as cover. Leader of the whole Randers network; refuses to flee after the arrests, is taken 30 Nov, and dies attacking a guard to escape a red-hot-stove interrogation.

**JØRGEN RØJEL, 27 — the doctor** ★
Junior doctor at Randers Hospital since August 1943, ordered by SOE to plan the bridges and stay away from every other illegal circle. Goes to bed convinced the fuses have failed, hears the first blast from his pillow, flees to Sweden two days later, and lives to 90 and writes the books everyone else quotes.

**SVEN CHRISTIAN JOHANNESEN, 20 — the young leader** †
Machinist at Scandia railway works, from Odense, Conservative Youth, leader of two earlier sabotage groups. Draws a pistol when the Gestapo break in, tells the court-martial his death will raise new fighters, hides a farewell note under a stamp: "My God, My Country, My Honour."

**OLUF AKSELBO KROER, 27 — the banker** †
Savings-bank clerk, Conservative Youth board member, old pupil of Hoff's school. Walks into a Gestapo trap carrying two pistols, one of them a policeman's from the bridge; writes to his mother that he goes to death a believing man.

**OLE HOVEDSKOV, 20 — the violinist**
Professional violinist, Conservative Youth, younger brother of the painter Børge. Arrested 30 Nov, sentenced to death May 1944, reprieved to life in a German prison, rescued by the Swedish Red Cross in 1945, comes home with tuberculosis and dies of it in 1951 at 28. Almost nothing else is known: maximum creative room.

**EJVIND JACOBSEN, 29 — "Ravn" (the Raven)** ★
Municipal schoolteacher, FDF scout leader, runs a parachute-reception group. Stays put for seven months under the Germans' noses, then escapes down a too-short paper rope from a second-floor balcony, breaks his foot, and is smuggled across Jutland to Sweden. Post-war city leader in Randers.

## B. THE OTHERS EXECUTED AT SKÆRING HEDE — 2 December 1943

**ANDERS WILLIAM ANDERSEN, 19** †
Randers boy, Conservative Youth and FDF, member of Hoff's Terrain Sports Club. Not on the bridge; arrested at the dairy on 19 Nov and, told his friend has already confessed, gives the interrogator Sven Johannesen's name.

**OTTO MANLEY CHRISTIANSEN, 19** †
Timber-yard worker, Conservative Youth and FDF, in an illegal-newspaper group and a sabotage group. Not on the bridge; the first thread the Gestapo pull, on the morning of 19 Nov, and the one that unravels the rest.

**GEORG MØRCH CHRISTIANSEN, 22** †
Economics student at Aarhus University, from Vejle, Aarhus group. Shot alongside the four from Randers; a stranger to them until the death cell.

**PER MOESGAARD-NIELSEN, 17–18, and KAJ SØRENSEN, 17–18**
The two youngest, caught almost by accident (Per ran down a staircase and it looked like flight). Spared by the court because of their age: life imprisonment in Germany.

**VIGGO TORSTENSSON**
Journalist trainee, arrested with Ole Hovedskov on 30 Nov, sentenced to death with him in May 1944, reprieved with him. Shares Ole's whole arc except the illness.

## C. THE PLANNERS AND HELPERS IN RANDERS

**OLE GEISLER — SOE captain**
Parachuted British organiser who gives Røjel the order to hit the bridges and sets off around fifteen sabotages across Jutland in the same days. Attends every Hvidsten drop; his tolerance of parachutists lodging at the inn is the crack the Gestapo later widen.

**DR. THORUP PETERSEN — the driver**
Randers physician with a car, drives Hoff and Røjel to reconnoitre, drives the explosives to the plantation on 15 Nov. It is he who warns Røjel on the morning of 20 Nov that the arrests have begun.

**CHRISTIAN BAK — the "sabotage technician"**
Municipal engineer who calculates that 75 kg of 808 on the bearings and main girders will bring the bridges down. Goes with the explosives to the burial site; a man who does the maths and then goes home.

**BØRGE HOVEDSKOV, 33 — the painter**
Ole's elder brother, Academy-trained painter and sculptor, also in the resistance (sources disagree on whether he was at Langå). Escapes to Sweden, founds an art school in Gothenburg, later designs a memorial to his dead comrades while his brother dies slowly.

**OTTO WESTERGÅRD OLESEN — the bookseller**
Runs Køsters Boghandel in Randers, where the parachuted weapons are stored before distribution. Arrested, Frøslev, then Dachau. Survives.

**THE HELPFUL POLICEMAN (unnamed)**
Danish officer who supplies the guard rota, post positions and shift times for the bridge. May be the same man as Carl Pedersen below, or someone we invent.

## D. AT THE BRIDGE THAT NIGHT

**THE TWO RESERVE POLICE GUARDS (unnamed)**
In on the plan. One whistles "Det var en lørdag aften" as the all-clear when a stray shot nearly aborts the mission, having already phoned the main guard to check. They surrender their pistols for appearances, are tied to a post out of blast range, and one of their pistols later hangs Oluf Kroer. What happened to them afterwards is a loose thread.

**RESERVE POLICE OFFICER NIELS GUNNAR PEDERSEN**
Buried in the memorial grove beside Kai Hoff in June 1945. Sources don't say how he died or whether he was one of the bridge guards. Worth chasing.

## E. LANGÅ — THE TOWN

**KNUD BIRK, 18–19 — the mechanic's son** ★
Only child of the Væthvej bicycle shop, standing on Langå platform with his toolbox that morning when a railway sleeper flies over the town. Not involved in the bridges but later builds bombs in his bedroom, cycles them to the Viborg line, escapes to Sweden. The natural "old man on the bench" for the framing device.

**NIELS BIRK — the father**
Blacksmith, ex-motorcycle racer, foreman at the Rekord machine factory, contracted to repair the dam contractor's machines. When an informer comes asking about his son he says the boy is on a course in Copenhagen.
*(Note: the research notes say he was "forced to work for the Nazis". The scanned pages only say he had a repair contract with the Danish dam contractor. Decide whether we keep the invented version.)*

**ULLA BIRK — the mother**
Runs the bicycle shop herself: patches, spokes, tyres, paints frames. The person who keeps everyone in Langå moving during rationing.

**THE LANGÅ INFORMER (unnamed)**
Local man who visits Niels Birk fishing for news of Knud. Liquidated by the resistance near the war's end. A whole subplot in one sentence.

**HANS NISSEN and LAUST JENSEN**
Woodwork-factory owner and his truck driver, caught collecting a compromised weapons drop at Tange Sø in 1944. Driver to Frøslev, owner to a German concentration camp.

**THE GERMAN OFFICERS ON THE PLATFORM**
Walking out to inspect the damage when the 9 o'clock charge goes off; run back to the station. The only comic beat in the sources.

## F. THE HUNTERS

**HARVY JENSEN, 22 — "Eriksen", the interpreter**
Danish civilian hired as Gestapo interpreter 36 hours before Langå. Friendly, brilliant at reading people, uses the "your friend already confessed" trick on teenagers; his work leads to 18 executions. Later warns the resistance of raids; gets 18 years, out in 1950. Røjel spent 27 years calling him a traitor and then, after reading the file, wasn't sure.

**"JACOB JENSEN" — the SOE agent who broke** (different Jensen)
British-trained Danish parachutist captured in Aarhus 13 Dec 1943. Under torture gives up the whole Jutland receiver network: 145 arrests, Hvidsten destroyed. Used as a prosecution witness; vanishes from the record after the war.

**DR. WERNER BEST — Reich plenipotentiary**
Wants a quiet, productive Denmark and as little German violence as possible. Reports to Berlin that the saboteurs are already caught and asks that no reprisals be taken against the population. The reason Langå was not Lidice.

**GÜNTHER PANCKE — Higher SS and Police Leader**
Promises Himmler that 24 arrested saboteurs will be tried and shot within days. The pressure behind the fourteen-day turnaround from bridge to firing squad.

**GENERAL HERMANN VON HANNEKEN — Wehrmacht commander**
The military chief, at odds with Best and Pancke. Represents the army's view: the bridge is a supply line to 350,000 men in Norway.

**THE COURT-MARTIAL JUDGES, THE DEFENCE COUNSEL, FELDPRÄST PLATE**
Three SS judges; a defender who pleads the ages of the two youngest and wins; a German army chaplain who takes a last communion with the condemned. Figures for the trial and execution scenes.

**THE FIRING SQUAD**
About 50 Wehrmacht soldiers in transit from the Russian front to Norway, pulled off a train to shoot five Danes on a heath at dawn. They are the reason the bridge mattered, meeting the men who blew it.

**OTTO SCHWERDT — "Peter Schäfer"** (later)
Skorzeny's number two at the Mussolini rescue, sent by Himmler to run a terror unit in Denmark from December 1943. Murders Kaj Munk a month after Skæring. Sentenced to death, freed in 1953, dies in Cologne.

## G. THE SUPPLY CHAIN — HVIDSTEN

**MARIUS FIIL — the innkeeper** †
Owner of Hvidsten Kro, leader of the receiver group that collects the Halifax drops, including the 80 kg for Langå on 15 Nov. Arrested at dawn 11 Mar 1944 with his son Niels and son-in-law Peder Sørensen; all three shot at Ryvangen in June.

**KIRSTINE and GERDA FIIL — the daughters**
Life imprisonment and two years respectively. Kirstine survives to see her father, brother and husband reburied at Hvidsten.

**FLEMMING JUNCKER — the landowner**
Master of Overgaard estate, the organiser who put Jutland's resistance together and offered his own fields for drops. Above the story, pulling strings.

**JENS TOLDSTRUP (Anton Ingersøn Jensen)** ★
Tax collector turned commander of all air-drop reception in Jutland. Runs a courier network of women known as "Toldstrup's girls".

**CARL and GERTRUD PEDERSEN**
Randers police officer and his wife, one of Toldstrup's girls, who work with Ejvind Jacobsen on the drops and are alerted, via a butcher, when he goes on the run.

## H. AFTER

**PASTOR POUL BORCHSENIUS**
Forbidden to speak at Kai Hoff's night-time burial, so he sings instead. Next day the townspeople cover the grave in red and white flowers.

**KAJ MUNK — the poet** †
Writes "Drenge, I drenge som døde" for the fallen; murdered by the Peter Group on 4 Jan 1944. His verse is on the stone at Skæring Hede.

**DIRECTOR WÆRUM, Scandia**
Sven Johannesen's employer. Refuses to sign the clemency petition: no punishment too hard for people who destroy Danish property. The town's other opinion, in one man.

**STIFTSPROVST KAJ JENSEN**
Dean who visits the five in the death cell and gives each a New Testament.

**THE GERMAN NCO WHO BURIED THEM**
Comes forward after the war to say where the bodies are: 500 m west of Husbjerg on the Oksbøl training ground. Six coffins are dug up and sent home.

## I. THE LONDON / WASHINGTON TIER (Narrative Device #2)

**HENRIK KAUFFMANN** — Danish ambassador in Washington, refuses orders from occupied Copenhagen, signs Greenland to the Americans, is charged with treason at home, signs the UN Charter for Denmark in 1945.

**CHRISTMAS MØLLER** — Conservative politician in London, voice of the BBC Danish Service, lobbies the British relentlessly for recognition.

**C.F.A. WARNER** (Foreign Office) and **HUGH CUMMING** (State Dept) — the two diplomats whose letters decide whether Denmark is "Allied", "associated", or neither. Our "men in the wood-panelled room".

**ROOSEVELT, CHURCHILL, STALIN at Yalta** — Roosevelt proposes Denmark for San Francisco; Churchill objects; Stalin agrees with Churchill.

**VAGN BENNIKE** — Jutland resistance commander whose desperate telegram brings the Mosquitos down on Gestapo HQ in Aarhus, 31 Oct 1944.

## J. THE FRAMING DEVICE (to be invented)

**THE CYCLIST** — punctures at the bridge; audience surrogate. Age, gender, and how much they already know are open.

**THE OLD MAN ON THE BENCH** — Knud Birk, or a fictional composite. Carries both the heroes' story and the town's compromises.

**OLE'S GIRL** — the love interest the notes call for. Musician, or a Randers girl; waits or doesn't; six years of slow goodbye.

**THE TOWN AS CHORUS** — kebab shop, library, bridgeheads, Skæring: each stop a different keeper of a fragment.

## FLAGGED DISCREPANCIES IN THE SOURCES
1. The research notes say Anders Andersen and Otto Manley Christiansen were "also executed"; Straarup's article makes clear they were in other Randers groups and NOT on the bridge. The film should decide whether to keep that distinction.
2. Ages: notes give Hoff 31 and Jacobsen "unknown"; Straarup gives Hoff 30, Jacobsen 29.
3. Explosive: info board says 80 kg; Bak calculated 75 kg of "808" (not plastic PE2).
4. Niels Birk "forced to work for the Nazis" is not in the scanned text (see E).
5. Two different Jensens: Harvy (interpreter, Randers) and "Jacob" (SOE agent, Aarhus). The notes conflate the arrests; Straarup credits Harvy Jensen with the Langå round-up, "Jacob" with Hvidsten.
6. Straarup says the two policemen were tied to a post after the charges were laid; the info board says before. Minor, but it changes the scene.
