# THE GERMAN OFFICER — Composite character, pass 1

Working name: **KRIMINALRAT ERNST VOGEL**, SS-Sturmbannführer, Sicherheitspolizei.
(Name is a placeholder. "Vogel" echoes General Vogel von Falckenstein, who blew the same bridge in 1864. Change it if that feels too neat.)

He is one man in the film. In reality he is three, plus a level above him. This document separates what is documented from what we take from each.

---

## 1. THE HISTORICAL FIGURES HE IS BUILT FROM

### EUGEN SCHWITZGEBEL (1900–1944) — the spine
- Kriminalrat and SS-Hauptsturmführer (later Sturmbannführer), chief of Gestapo Jutland.
- Arrived Aarhus October 1943, six weeks before Langå, and set up in dormitories 4 and 5 at Aarhus University.
- Described as a short, stocky Bavarian, ruthless, impatient, who loved to terrorise his subordinates. "Remarkably energetic" at recruiting Danish informants.
- Led raids in person. 13 January 1944 he led the raid on the Kæraas villa in Risskov that shot a woman dead in front of her seven-year-old.
- Received Kaj Munk's murderers as honoured guests in January 1944 and proposed to hire them for reprisal bombings and killings in Aarhus.
- Killed at his desk on 31 October 1944 when 25 Mosquitos flattened the dormitories. Buried at Vestre Kirkegård, Copenhagen.
- We take: the base, the rank, the energy, the informant-recruiting, the death.

### HARVY JENSEN, alias "ERIKSEN" (b. 1921) — the face
- Danish. Twenty-two. Hired as Gestapo interpreter 36 hours before he was driven to Langå.
- Not a torturer. A listener. "Incredibly good at putting things together." Asked teenagers about their families and their clubs, seemed trustworthy, told each one the last one had already confessed. Broke the Randers groups in 48 hours. His work led to 18 executions.
- Late in the war he warned the resistance about coming raids. Sentenced to 18 years, released 1950.
- Røjel called him a traitor for 27 years, read the case file in 2000, and wasn't sure any more.
- We take: the method. In the film, the officer does what Eriksen did. The interpreter stays as a separate small character, or is folded in. Decide in the room.

### THE TRUPPEN-ORTSKOMMANDANT, RANDERS — the social man
- The Wehrmacht garrison commander. Documented as existing (the Statsskole was warned by the Truppenortskommandantur in 1944) but not named in our sources.
- The man who would actually sit in a hotel dining room. Army, not SS. Older. The kind who had a decent war until November 1943.
- We take: the dinner table, the culture, the humiliation. Someone in Randers was responsible for the security of that bridge, and on 18 November his career ended.

### ABOVE HIM: BEST, PANCKE, VON HANNEKEN — the three who go to Hitler
- Werner Best, civilian plenipotentiary, wants a quiet Denmark. Pancke, Higher SS and Police Leader, wants executions. Von Hanneken, army commander, wants the railway open. They dislike each other.
- On 30 December 1943 all three are summoned to the Wolfsschanze. Himmler and Kaltenbrunner are in the room. (DOCUMENTED, from the Best trial transcript.)

## 2. THE COMPOSITE

**Who he is in the film.** A German policeman of the professional kind. Kriminalrat is a civil police rank: he was a detective in Munich before he was ever SS. He is 43. He likes music, genuinely, and knows enough to know Ole is good. He has been in Denmark since 1940 and has spent three years being polite in a country that was polite back. He believes, on 16 November 1943, that he understands the Danes.

**What he wants.** Order. Specifically: he wants Denmark to stay the one posting in Europe where a policeman can do his job without becoming a butcher. He is not a good man. He is a man who has arranged his life so that he never has to find out what he is.

**What Langå does to him.** It takes that away. The bridge falls on his watch. Pancke wants heads within days. Berlin is asking questions. He has a choice: do this the police way, slowly and properly, or the SS way. He chooses the police way and it works, horribly well: a 22-year-old Danish interpreter and a kind voice break the whole network in two days, without a single blow. Five boys are shot on a heath a fortnight later. He has proven that you don't need torture. He has also proven that he will deliver five corpses on schedule when asked.

**The turn.** Hoff. 30 November. Hoff won't talk to the kind voice. The stove is lit. Our officer is in the room, or has left the room, and that distinction is the moral centre of his story. When Hoff is shot in the back through the window, something in the officer decides that he is, after all, exactly what the uniform says.

**Then Hitler takes even that away.** Thirty days later at the Wolfsschanze the Führer says that trials only create martyrs, and orders murder for murder, sabotage for sabotage, against purely Danish targets: newspaper offices, sports halls, places of entertainment. The policeman's whole method is abolished from above. Five days later Kaj Munk is in a ditch. In January the murderers come through Aarhus and the officer, like Schwitzgebel, gives them dinner.

**His end.** 31 October 1944, 11.30 in the morning, at his desk in dormitory 4, when the Mosquitos arrive. The audience knows the telegram that brought them was sent because of the chain that started at Langå. He doesn't.

## 3. THE DINING ROOM (proposed setting)

**Hotel Randers**, Torvegade. Founded 1856, Denmark's oldest provincial hotel, an Art Deco room that won a prize at the 1934 Copenhagen exhibition. (Not documented as German-used in our sources. We are inventing this. Hotel Corner on Storegade is documented as the SS recruiting office and was the resistance's first target, if we want a nastier venue.)

Autumn 1943. Saturday dinner. The dining room half German officers, half the kind of Randers bourgeoisie who still come. Ole and Inger play at the far end: light classical, some Danish songs, a little Lehár for the guests. Ole hates it and needs the money. The officer requests something. Schubert, say. Ole plays it well. The officer sends over a glass of something and a card, and tells Inger, in good Danish, that her partner should be in Berlin. That card is in Ole's pocket on 17 November. It is still in his pocket on 30 November.

**Why this scene matters.** It puts the two of them in the same room, on opposite sides of the same music, before either knows who the other is. Everything after is that scene curdling.

## 4. THE HITLER SCENE — what is documented

**30 December 1943, Führerhauptquartier Wolfsschanze, East Prussia.** Present: Hitler, Himmler, Kaltenbrunner, Best, von Hanneken, Pancke.

- Hitler asked Best how a small country like Denmark could not be controlled. (Danish Wikipedia, Langåbrosprængningerne.)
- Best argued Denmark's economic importance, said the security situation was favourable, and recommended faster court procedures. Pancke asked for SS and police courts.
- Hitler rejected both. Jurisdiction, he said, "blot ville skabe martyrer": would only create martyrs.
- He ordered that every killing of a German or German-friendly person be answered immediately and under the same conditions, and sabotage answered with sabotage against "rene danske objekter: bladhuse, sportsindretninger, forlystelsessteder".
- Ratio stated as five to one. Pancke later proposed one to one.
- Best and Pancke both protested afterwards that this would destabilise the country. They were right and it didn't matter.
- 4 January 1944: Kaj Munk murdered by Schwerdt's group. Body in a ditch near Silkeborg with a note pinned to it.

The scene writes itself, and we don't have to invent the substance. What we invent is the temperature of the room, and the moment Best realises Skæring Hede, which he thought was the answer, was the thing that made Hitler ask the question.

## 5. OPEN DECISIONS

1. **One man or two?** The composite officer AND a separate Eriksen (the boy interpreter), or fold Eriksen into him? Two gives us a scene where the officer watches a 22-year-old Dane do his job better than he can. Recommend two.
2. **Is he in the room when Hoff dies?** Recommend: he leaves. It is worse.
3. **Does he ever find out Ole was on the bridge?** He arrested him. Recommend he recognises the violinist in the cell, and that this is the one interrogation he conducts himself, and that he is gentle, and that it is the most frightening scene in the film.
4. **Name.** Vogel is a placeholder.
