# LANGÅ — SCENE LIST, draft 1

Three strands, cut against each other:
- **NOW** — the cyclist and the old man, Langå, present day.
- **THEN** — Randers, Langå, Aarhus, Copenhagen, Germany: September 1943 to December 1951.
- **ABOVE** — the rooms where Denmark's fate is argued: Wolfsschanze, London, Washington, Yalta, San Francisco.

Tags: [DOC] documented in our sources. [INV] invented. [MIX] documented event, invented detail.
Scene numbers are for reference only; the order will move.

---

## ACT ONE — THE SPRING HE THINKS HE HAS

**1. NOW — The puncture.** [INV]
Gudenå valley, summer. A cyclist on the old railway embankment. The tyre goes at the rust-red bridge. An old man on the bench watches them fail to fix it.

**2. NOW — "Which bridge?"** [INV]
The old man says the bridge has been blown up twice. The cyclist thought there was one bridge. He points: there were three. That one survived both times. Story begins.

**3. THEN — Aarhus–Randers train, September 1943.** [INV]
Ole, violin case on his knee, eyes closed. The sound changes as the wheels leave the embankment and hit iron. He knows the bridge by its pitch. We see the three bridges from a moving train.

**4. THEN — 29 August, in retrospect.** [DOC/MIX]
Randers. The Germans disarm the Danish army; 23 soldiers die elsewhere refusing. The Statsskole boys talk of nothing else. Kai Hoff, teaching Latin, says nothing at all. Sven Johannesen at Scandia watches a train of German equipment roll north.

**5. THEN — Hotel Randers, Saturday night.** [INV]
Ole and Inger play the dining room. Half the tables are grey uniforms. Kriminalrat Vogel requests Schubert. Ole plays it beautifully and hates every note. Vogel sends over a card: "You should be in Berlin." Ole pockets it.

**6. THEN — The walk home.** [INV]
Inger: "You took his card." Ole: "I took his money too." First articulation of the compromise. She is not angry. That is worse.

**7. THEN — Hvidsten Kro, August.** [DOC]
Røjel, new junior doctor at Randers Hospital, at the inn. Ole Geisler, SOE, tells him: plan the Langå bridges, and stay away from this inn and everyone in it from now on. Compartments.

**8. THEN — Terrænsportsforeningen.** [MIX]
Hoff's terrain-sports club on a Sunday: young men running through woods with rucksacks. Recruitment without the word. Sven, Anders, Otto, Per. Hoff watches who doesn't complain.

**9. THEN — The question that isn't a question.** [INV]
KU dance. Sven Johannesen and Ole outside, smoking. Sven asks something oblique. Ole says yes before it's finished. He tells himself it's for Denmark.

**10. THEN — Børge.** [INV]
Copenhagen or Randers. The brothers. Børge already in; Børge who is always already in. Ole doesn't tell him he's said yes. First time in his life he has a secret from his brother.

**11. THEN — The failed locomotives.** [DOC]
October. Hoff, Røjel and Thorup Petersen reconnoitre the Langå engine shed. The Saturday raid on ten locomotives is aborted: only two engines, and two men crashed in the dark. They blow an iron foundry instead. First taste of how things go wrong.

**12. THEN — The helpful policeman.** [DOC/MIX]
A Danish constable hands over the bridge guard rota, post positions, shift changes. Sketches of the bridges from "friends of friends". Engineer Christian Bak does the arithmetic on a kitchen table: 75 kg on the bearings and main girders.

**13. THEN — Køsters Boghandel.** [DOC]
Weapons and explosive under the floor of a Randers bookshop. Otto Westergård Olesen sells a schoolbook to a German soldier over the top of it.

**14. ABOVE — BBC Danish Service, London.** [DOC]
"Hilsen til Elias. Lyt igen." Christmas Møller at the microphone. In a Randers kitchen, Wilhelmine Hovedskov hears it and doesn't know what it means. Ole does.

**15. THEN — Mustard Point, night of 15 November.** [DOC/MIX]
Halifax over Hvidsten fields. Marius Fiil and his family with torches. Containers in the mud. Eighty kilos of Nobel 808, smelling of almonds.

**16. THEN — The plantation.** [DOC]
Thorup Petersen, Bak and Røjel drive two crates to a plantation north-west of the bridges and bury them. Two days to go.

**17. THEN — Ole's hands.** [INV]
Ole practises the Sibelius for the spring audition. Inger at the piano. He stops mid-phrase. "If something happened to my hands." She: "Then you'd conduct." A joke. It isn't.

## ACT TWO — THE NIGHT AND THE FORTNIGHT

**18. THEN — Six bicycles, Wednesday evening, 17 November.** [DOC]
Randers to Langå in sleet. Six men who mostly don't know each other's names. Hoff and Røjel in front with pistols and "pineapples". Ole at the back with the violinist's hands in borrowed gloves.

**19. THEN — The shot.** [DOC]
Two hundred metres from the bridges: a single shot from the dark. Thirty seconds of certainty that they are surrounded. Røjel decides to go on and blow the bridges before they're taken.

**20. THEN — A quarter-tone flat.** [MIX]
Someone whistles "Det var en lørdag aften". Ole: "It's him. He always whistles it flat." The constable has already phoned the main guard: the shot was a mistake. The musician's ear clears the mission.

**21. THEN — The guards.** [DOC]
For appearances: pistols taken, rifles emptied and thrown in the river against fingerprints. Two Danish policemen who agreed to this weeks ago, now genuinely afraid. One of those pistols will hang Oluf Kroer.

**22. THEN — Clay in the dark.** [DOC/MIX]
Kneading 808 onto bearings and girders with frozen fingers. Detonators. Cordtex. Six charges on three bridges. The almond smell. Ole's headache starting behind the eyes.

**23. THEN — The pencils.** [DOC]
Thirty-minute time pencils, rated at fifteen degrees. Snow falling. Nobody says that it is a lot colder than fifteen degrees. The guards tied to a post out of blast range.

**24. THEN — Home.** [DOC/MIX]
Røjel in bed, awake, thinking it has failed. Ole washing the almond off in the kitchen; his mother's bedroom door, closed, a line of light under it.

**25. THEN — 5.25.** [DOC/MIX]
Ole feels it in the bedframe before he hears it. Røjel hears it and, in his own words, is relieved. Across Randers, six men lying still in the dark, counting.

**26. THEN — Langå station, morning of 18 November.** [DOC]
Knud Birk, nineteen, with his toolbox, waiting to go out to the dam. German officers set off for the bridges on foot. 9.41: the last charge. A railway sleeper flies over the town and lands in the plant nursery. The officers run back to the platform.

**27. THEN — The old bridge.** [DOC]
Knud cycles out, crosses on the 1862 box bridge, which is barely scratched, and looks back at two lattice bridges sagging into the Gudenå. Trains north stop for twelve days.

**28. NOW — The sleeper.** [INV]
The old man, on the bench: "It landed there." Points. A garden centre. The cyclist looks at a garden centre.

**29. THEN — Vogel at the bridge.** [INV]
The Kriminalrat, in a good coat, standing on the embankment. Aarhus has had the Jutland Gestapo for six weeks. This is his first real case. Behind him, a young Dane in a borrowed overcoat: Harvy Jensen, hired 36 hours ago. "Eriksen".

**30. ABOVE — Berlin, 20 November.** [DOC]
Pancke's telegram to Himmler: 24 saboteurs will be tried and shot within days. Best's addendum: the security police is working excellently; he asks that no reprisals be taken against the population. Two men in one telegram, pulling opposite ways.

**31. THEN — Eriksen at the timber yard, 19 November, 10.30.** [DOC]
Otto Manley Christiansen, nineteen, at work. A friendly Dane asks about his family, his clubs. Says it'll all be fine if he tells everything. Otto tells everything.

**32. THEN — The dairy.** [DOC]
Anders Andersen, nineteen. "Otto's already told us about your sabotage together." Oldest trick there is. Anders gives them Sven's name. Per Moesgaard-Nielsen runs down a staircase at the wrong moment and is taken for nothing.

**33. THEN — Søren Møllersgade 27, 17.00.** [DOC]
Sven Johannesen draws a pistol at his own door. Overpowered. On him: a second pistol, from the bridge.

**34. THEN — 18.00, after closing.** [DOC]
Oluf Kroer locks the Sparekasse, walks to Sven's with two pistols in his coat, rings the bell, and is pulled inside by two Gestapo men. One pistol has a police serial number.

**35. THEN — The notebook.** [DOC]
Aarhus prison. Oluf writes: first I denied everything, but when I learned Johannesen had confessed, I had to show my colours. The Germans were considerably nicer than I expected. He is writing for a reader in a uniform.

**36. THEN — Vogel and Eriksen.** [INV]
The Kriminalrat watches the boy interpreter work through a glass door. Forty-eight hours, no blows, the whole network. Vogel is impressed and something else: a professional watching himself made obsolete by a twenty-two-year-old.

**37. THEN — Saturday 20 November, dawn.** [DOC]
Thorup Petersen wakes Røjel: they've started. Røjel is out of Randers within the hour. Ravn decides no one knows him and stays. Hoff goes to school on Monday.

**38. THEN — Ole's Saturday.** [INV]
He plays a dance that night, with Inger. He knows about Sven and Oluf from a paperboy. He plays anyway. He can't tell his father why he should leave, so he doesn't.

**39. THEN — Hoff teaches Latin.** [MIX]
Randers Statsskole. Hoff at the board, watched by boys who know, and by someone across the street who is watching him for the Germans, hoping he'll lead them to more.

**40. THEN — The court-martial, Aarhus, 24 November.** [DOC]
Six from Randers confirm their confessions. The four youngest express regret. Kroer and Johannesen refuse: they'd do it again. Sven tells three SS judges that after his death new men will rise; he is glad to show the way to those who hesitate. The defender pleads the ages of the two seventeen-year-olds and wins them their lives.

**41. THEN — 30 November, morning: Hoff.** [DOC]
Hoff's flat, full of weapons and explosive. Arrested. Thorsgade barracks. Hours standing at a hot stove. He grabs a chair, attacks a soldier, goes for the window. Shot in the back. Dies within hours.

**42. THEN — Vogel leaves the room.** [INV]
Before the stove. He gives no order. He just goes to get coffee. The film's moral centre is a man walking down a corridor.

**43. THEN — 30 November, afternoon: Ole.** [MIX]
Ole is practising. They knock. He asks to put the violin in its case first. They let him. Vogel's card is in his jacket pocket. Viggo Torstensson taken the same hour.

**44. THEN — The burial in the dark.** [DOC]
Østre Kirkegård, night. Pastor Borchsenius is forbidden to speak. He sings "Altid frejdig" instead. Next morning the grave and everything around it is covered in red and white flowers.

**45. THEN — Death cell, Aarhus, 1 December.** [DOC]
Stiftsprovst Kaj Jensen brings five New Testaments. Sven, Oluf, Anders, Otto, and a stranger from Aarhus, Georg, who is now their brother.

**46. THEN — 2 December, 5 a.m.** [DOC]
Woken. Two hours. Letters. Otto: "Mor, du skal have mit armbåndsur." Oluf: "Jeg går i døden som en troende mand." Sven's letter is confiscated as too persuasive; he writes the King's motto on the back of a stamp. Feldpräst Plate gives communion.

**47. THEN — Skæring Hede.** [DOC]
Dawn over the bay. Five stakes. About fifty Wehrmacht soldiers pulled off a train from Russia to Norway: the exact traffic the bridge was blown to stop. All five calm. White coffins. The bodies vanish.

**48. THEN — Randers, 2 December, afternoon.** [DOC]
Every flag at half mast. Director Wærum at Scandia refuses to sign the clemency petition: no punishment too hard for men who destroy Danish property. The town's two faces in one day.

**49. THEN — Ole hears.** [INV]
A guard tells him about Hoff because he thinks Ole will enjoy it. He learns about Skæring from the silence in the wing.

**50. THEN — The violinist and the policeman.** [INV]
Vogel recognises him. Conducts this one interrogation himself. Is gentle. Asks about Schubert. It is the most frightening scene in the film, and Ole says nothing, out of a stubbornness he never knew he had.

**51. THEN — 13 December: the other Jensen.** [DOC]
Aarhus. SOE parachutist "Jacob Jensen" captured. Under torture, he gives up every receiver group in Jutland. The chain that will reach Hvidsten starts here.

**52. ABOVE — Wolfsschanze, 30 December 1943.** [DOC]
Best, Pancke, von Hanneken before Hitler; Himmler and Kaltenbrunner at the wall. "How can a small country like Denmark not be controlled?" Best: faster trials. Pancke: SS courts. Hitler: trials only create martyrs. Murder for murder, sabotage for sabotage, against purely Danish targets: newspapers, sports halls, entertainment. Five for one. Best realises that Skæring, his proof of efficiency, is the thing that made the Führer ask.

**53. THEN — 4 January 1944.** [DOC]
Kaj Munk, who wrote the verse for the five, in a ditch near Silkeborg with a note pinned to his coat. Schwerdt's men drive on to Aarhus, where the Kriminalrat gives them dinner.

## ACT THREE — THE LONG NOTE

**54. THEN — Vestre Fængsel, spring 1944.** [MIX]
Ole in the death cell, Copenhagen. Sentenced 16 May with Torstensson. No instrument. He plays the Sibelius on his forearm with his left hand and hears every note.

**55. THEN — Hvidsten, 11 March, dawn.** [DOC]
Gestapo around the inn. Marius Fiil, his son, his son-in-law, his daughters. The people who collected the eighty kilos.

**56. THEN — Ravn's rope, 9 June, 5.40 p.m.** [DOC]
Drosselhaven, Randers. Gestapo at the front door. Ejvind Jacobsen out the balcony door on a paper rope that reaches only the first floor. He drops. Foot broken. A butcher hides him. Carl and Gertrud Pedersen get him out across Jutland.

**57. THEN — 29 June.** [DOC/MIX]
Vestre Fængsel. Eight Hvidsten men taken out at dawn. Ole hears the wing. Then nothing. The executions stop and no one tells him why.

**58. THEN — A summer of dawns.** [INV]
Ole waits for a morning that doesn't come. He never learns that an innkeeper's family bought him his life.

**59. THEN — 19 September: "livsvarigt".** [DOC]
A piece of paper. Life. Germany.

**60. THEN — Dreibergen-Bützow, winter.** [MIX]
Mecklenburg. Cold, hunger. A cough in January that doesn't stop. He stops playing on his forearm because the fingers won't close.

**61. ABOVE — The telegram.** [DOC]
Vagn Bennike to London: the resistance in Jutland is about to be torn up by the Gestapo. I insistently ask that dormitories 4 and 5 be destroyed from the air.

**62. THEN — 31 October 1944, 11.30.** [DOC/INV]
Twenty-five Mosquitos at rooftop height over Aarhus. Vogel at his desk in dormitory 4. Archives burning. He never knew the chain began at Langå.

**63. ABOVE — Whitehall, 1944.** [DOC/MIX]
Warner writes to Cumming. "Associated with the United Nations." Three worries: would the Danes even want it, there's a legation in Berlin, and what will the Russians say. A nation's soul in a civil-service letter.

**64. ABOVE — Yalta, February 1945.** [DOC]
Roosevelt: what about Denmark at San Francisco? Churchill objects. Stalin agrees with Churchill. One sentence, and Denmark is out.

**65. THEN — The white buses, April 1945.** [DOC/MIX]
A Swedish bus with a red cross on the roof at a German prison gate. Ole doesn't believe it until Malmö.

**66. THEN — The platform.** [INV]
Randers. Inger. Wilhelmine. Peter Valdemar has brought the violin. Ole cannot lift his arm to take it.

**67. THEN — May 1945: the graves.** [DOC]
A German NCO tells where they are: 500 m west of Husbjerg on the Oksbøl range. Six coffins. 25 May: Anders, Oluf and Sven come home to Mindelunden. 23 June: Hoff moved to lie beside them.

**68. ABOVE — San Francisco, 26 June 1945.** [DOC]
The conference itself votes to invite "newly liberated Denmark". Henrik Kauffmann, lately a traitor, signs the Charter. In the folder on the table: a report on a railway bridge.

**69. THEN — Vejlefjord.** [INV]
A bed on a veranda, sea air. Years. Inger visits. The disease advances and retreats.

**70. THEN — The brothers.** [INV]
Børge comes once, from Gothenburg, with drawings for a monument. The best-written scene in the film. Neither says the thing.

**71. THEN — The violin.** [INV]
Peter Valdemar sells it to pay for the sanatorium. Or Inger keeps it and never plays it. Decide.

**72. THEN — 8 December 1951.** [DOC/INV]
Ole, twenty-eight. Six years after the war. The last of the six to die of it. Buried in Mindelunden under Kaj Munk's verse, eight years late.

**73. NOW — Skæring Hede.** [INV]
The cyclist, tyre mended, has followed the old man's directions. A boulder, five oaks, the bay. The names.

**74. NOW — The bench.** [INV]
Back at the bridge. The old man is gone, or is Knud Birk, or is nobody. The cyclist puts a hand on rust-red iron and feels a train coming on the new bridge: the pitch changes as it leaves the embankment.

## NOTES FOR THE ROOM

- **Ratio.** Roughly 50 THEN, 12 ABOVE, 8 NOW. ABOVE may be too much; but scene 52 must stay.
- **The two Jensens** (Eriksen in Randers, "Jacob" in Aarhus) will confuse an audience. Either rename one in the film or make the confusion a beat.
- **Scene 5 needs a date.** Before 29 August the hotel scene is a compromise; after it, it is something worse. Recommend after.
- **What we haven't got:** anyone at Langå the night before, the German guard's reaction, the Fiil daughters after 1944, Knud Birk's own escape. All available for invention.
- **Missing thread:** the police guards after the war. Scene 21 sets it up; nothing pays it off yet.
